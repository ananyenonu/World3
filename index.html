A model, not a prophecy

In 1972, a team at MIT built World3 for the Club of Rome. It was not a forecast machine. It was a laboratory for asking: if growth continues, what does the system do next?

The model linked five sectors — population, capital, agriculture, resources and pollution — and ran them from 1900 to 2100. Its output was a family of scenarios, not a single future.

Systems that grow, and systems that push back

System dynamics models the world as stocks, flows and feedback loops. A stock is a bathtub; a flow is the tap. Feedback turns the tap based on the water level — but only after a delay.

Reinforcing loop (R)

More people → more workers and demand → more industrial output → more food, health and services → more people. Growth feeds itself.

Balancing loops (B)

More output → more resource extraction and pollution → lower fertility, food and health → slower growth. Limits push back.

The behaviour of the whole depends on which loop dominates — and on how long the signals take to travel.

Five sectors, one coupled machine

World3 is highly aggregated: one world population, one industrial capital stock, one pollution stock. That is its weakness and its power — it forces us to see the coupling.

Overshoot is what delay looks like

Three ingredients are enough: growth, a limit, and a lag between the signal and the response.

1

Growth is exponential while resources look abundant.

2

The limit declines as the system overshoots it: soil erodes, stocks deplete, sinks fill.

The delay means the response arrives after the limit has already been crossed.

The standard run: growth, peak, decline

“Business as usual” holds the relationships of 1900–1970 constant. Output keeps growing; resource extraction accelerates; capital is diverted to ever more expensive extraction; food and services tighten; death rates rise.

The causal chain

Resource depletion → higher extraction cost → less capital for agriculture and services → falling food and health → rising mortality → population decline.

Four futures, four assumptions

Historic relationships continue. Collapse is driven by non-renewable resource depletion.

All curves are indexed to 2000 = 100. For non-BAU scenarios, the dashed curve is the BAU reference; the solid curves show the selected scenario.

BAU · business as usual

No new assumptions. Resources deplete; growth overshoots and collapses in the mid-21st century.

BAU2 · twice the resources

More fossil fuel and minerals, plus recycling. Collapse is postponed — and caused by pollution instead.

CT · comprehensive technology

Aggressive pollution control, land yield and resource efficiency. Growth stalls; decline, but no full collapse.

SW · stabilised world

Technology plus changed values and policies: smaller families, output caps, more services. The only scenario without decline.

Technology alone changes the timing and cause. Changing the goal — from growth to sufficiency — changes the direction.

Read the dashed line

The dashed curve is always BAU, for comparison. A scenario that looks “better” on one variable can still be worse on another; the model is a package of assumptions, not a menu of independent fixes.

How do you test a world model?

Not by asking whether it “got 2020 right”. A world model is tested on whether its structure reproduces the direction, timing and turning points of observed trajectories — and whether it behaves sensibly when assumptions change.

1 · Narrative

Start with a story about how the world works: more capital produces more output; more output consumes resources and emits pollution; pollution harms health and land with a delay.

Two rulers, used together

NRMSD — normalised root-mean-square deviation across the whole series. One number per variable, comparable across sectors.

Value + rate of change — does the model match the level and the slope at the latest observation? Tolerance is 20% on the value, 50% on the rate of change, and 20% on NRMSD.

A good fit does not prove the model is true. A bad fit does not prove it is useless. The question is whether the mechanism earns its keep.

Thirty years of reality: the standard run holds

Turner compared 1970–2000 observations with three 1972 scenarios. The data were consistent with BAU, and much less consistent with comprehensive technology or a stabilised world.

Approximate gap at 2000: model vs observed

Population looks close, but only because birth and death rates offset each other. Industrial output within ~15%; food ~5%; pollution ~15%; resources close to the lower bound.

BAU consistent CT overshoots observations SW least consistent

“Thirty years of historical data compare favorably with key features of a business-as-usual scenario.”

Turner, 2008, abstract

Why this mattered

It rebutted the myth that LtG predicted collapse by 2000. The model's turning points lie in the 21st century; the data had only reached the rising edge.

The pollution warning

Turner concluded that understanding and controlling persistent pollution was the critical uncertainty — a theme that returns in every later comparison.

What was compared

Six aggregate series over 1970–2000, against three 1972 scenarios. The test is at the level of trajectories, not country detail — so it can support a behaviour mode, not a local forecast.

Fifty years of reality: the window is closing

Herrington repeated the comparison with World3-03, data to 2019, and two new variables: human welfare and the ecological footprint. The closest scenarios were BAU2 and CT — not the original BAU.

Closest-fit count across 10 variables

Both closest scenarios imply a halt in welfare, food and industrial growth within about a decade. Only BAU2 depicts a full collapse — driven by pollution, the climate-change equivalent.

What changed since Turner?

A revised model (World3-03), better data, two extra variables, and a different statistical treatment. The conclusion shifted from “BAU” to “BAU2 or CT” — but the direction did not: not the sustainable scenario.

The uncomfortable result

SW — the only scenario that avoids decline — had the lowest closest-fit count (3). Humanity has not been following the pathway that works.

“The window to align with this last scenario is closing.”

Herrington, 2021

Counts: BAU 4 · BAU2 6 · CT 7 · SW 3 · inconclusive 2. A scenario is counted only when both accuracy measures fall inside the uncertainty band.

What a count can and cannot say

The count is not a vote: variables are correlated, and a scenario can be closest on many weak indicators while missing the one that matters. That is why Herrington reports both NRMSD and the latest value / rate-of-change gap.

Calibrating the present: what changed by 2012?

Pasqualino and colleagues recalibrated World3-03 on 1995–2012 data, starting from Scenario 2. The exercise asks: what parameter values would make the model's recent behaviour match the world we actually measured?

Three signals from the calibration

Land yield factor1.00 → 1.63–1.67
Arable-land erosion factor0 → 0.92–0.94
Processing loss10% → 35%

Interpretation: more investment in pollution abatement, higher agricultural productivity, better land management, and a more productive service sector than the 1972 assumptions.

Read the fine print

The model was not run forward beyond 2012. This is a calibration of the growth phase, not evidence that collapse was avoided. The authors say so explicitly.

Scenario 2 calibrated, with limits calibrated, no limits

Why “with” and “without” limits?

The calibration is run twice: first with resource and pollution feedbacks switched off (to fit the growth phase), then with them restored. The difference is a measure of how much the omitted limits matter.

Sensitive numbers, robust story

In 1976, Vermeulen and de Jongh showed that World3 was very sensitive to small parameter changes. Altering three capital parameters by 10% in 1975 was enough to avert the standard run's collapse.

Maximum inflation factor · capital sector

Normalised sensitivity: % change in a state variable per 1% change in the parameter, maximum over 1900–2100. Values above 1 amplify; above 10 are extreme.

1976: the model is fragile

ICOR, FIOAC and ALIC were the pressure points. A 10% shift in the “optimistic” direction averted collapse; the opposite direction produced a worse disaster. The authors concluded that small, well-aimed pressures might change the world's path.

Today: the mode is robust

Later work replicated the perturbations and found that the timing, height and cause change — but overshoot and decline remain. Nebel's sensitivity runs all reproduce the same behaviour mode.

“It is the dynamics of the system that is most important, not the value of a particular parameter at a particular point in time.”

Nebel et al., 2024, §3.3

Both claims can be true at once. Parameter sensitivity undermines point forecasts; structural robustness supports risk statements. That is why we should talk about the shape of the future, not a single date.

Three structural findings

1. Population and industrial output are the most connected nodes. 2. The resource threshold decides whether the system falls into the terminal (collapse) basin. 3. Technology can restore a sector temporarily, but cannot repeal the limit.

What World3 gets right — and what it cannot do

The model is best judged as a reasoning tool: what it clarifies, where it overreaches, and how to use it without pretending that it can foretell the future.

Enduring lessons

  1. Limits are plural. Solve one and another becomes binding. There is no single “resource crisis”.
  2. Delays manufacture overshoot. A smooth signal today can hide a committed decline tomorrow.
  3. Exponential growth exhausts fixed stocks suddenly, not gradually. The last doubling is the whole story.
  4. Technology changes timing; values and institutions change direction. Only SW avoids decline, and it needs both.
  5. Models are arguments made explicit. They can be inspected, criticised and improved — unlike intuition.

Limits and critiques

  1. One aggregated world. No regions, trade, inequality or politics. A global average can hide a collapse next door.
  2. No price system or endogenous innovation. Scarcity does not automatically trigger substitution, as critics from Solow onward argued.
  3. Sensitive parameters, proxy data. CO₂ for pollution, industrial production index for output, education for services.
  4. Post-collapse trajectories are not credible. The equations hold social relationships constant — exactly what would change.
  5. Normative assumptions are embedded in structure: what counts as welfare, what technology can do, whose future is modelled.

The future is not predicted; it is chosen within limits

World3 does not tell us what will happen. It tells us what kind of system we are running: one where growth is fast, feedback is slow, and the bill for overshoot arrives later than the decision that caused it.

01

Slow the throughput

Reduce the rate at which materials and energy pass through the economy. Efficiency buys time; sufficiency changes the destination.

02

Shorten the delays

Measure what matters sooner, and act before the signal is unambiguous. Early response is cheaper than late repair.

03

Protect the buffers

Soil, freshwater, forests and climate are the capacity that makes recovery possible. Eroding them turns a dip into a collapse.

04

Invest in response capacity

Institutions, knowledge and social trust determine whether a society can change course while it still has options.

“Humanity can either choose its own limit or at some point reach an imposed limit, at which time a decline in human welfare will have become unavoidable.”

Herrington, 2021, paraphrasing the LtG message

For discussion

1. Which limit is binding now — and which one are we treating as background?

2. Where are the longest delays between cause and consequence in our own institutions?

3. If SW requires changed values, who gets to decide what “enough” means?

How the visuals were made

Charts are hand-built SVG. The standard-run and scenario curves are stylised reconstructions of published behaviour, not digitised data; the overshoot simulator is a toy model built to isolate the delay mechanism. Empirical values (NRMSD, closest-fit counts, parameter shifts, sensitivities) are taken from the papers cited.

Scope & caveats

This deck is an interpretation, not a systematic review. Values are rounded from the cited papers; curves are stylised; the 2024 recalibration uses the corrected 2025 values. The supplied papers are the evidence base; the two Limits to Growth books and the Eden3 work supply context. Neither list is the whole literature.

Reproducibility

World3-03 has an open-source implementation in PyWorld3-03, and the 2024 study releases its optimisation code and data. Reproducing a run is the best way to see how assumptions move the output.
